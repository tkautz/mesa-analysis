School Attendance Area Boundary Policy 4
Reasons for Changing Attendance Boundaries
● Opening a new school, changing a neighborhood school to
non-neighborhood (focus), or closing a school
● Fix a specific inefficiency or impracticality
● Balance base residential student population among schools
● Attempt to optimize resources
● Goal of fostering community cohesion
● Attempt to address demographic shifts or rebalancing composition
School Attendance Area Boundary Policy 5
Limitations of Changing Boundaries
● Can only redistribute base resident populations, not overall enrollment
● Enrollment is a function of many factors besides resident population,
including open enrollment, program options, and community values
● Innately disruptive to families and neighborhoods (siblings, home
purchases, attachment to a school community)
● Unintended consequences (e.g. community division, transportation
impacts)
● Concessions may water down gains in short term
● Not a precise tool in eliminating inequalities
● Some stakeholders will inevitably be dissatisfied
