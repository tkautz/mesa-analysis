## 2016-02-09 — 8.1 2015 Enrollment Update  (item A6KTQY78855F; Regular Meeting)

Attachments: (3) Out of District Students 2015(P9).pdf; (4) Births By Elementary Attendance Area.pdf; (1) BVSD Enrollment Growth (1).pdf; BOE Enrollment 2015 Slides-BOE.pdf; (2) Enrollment Growth by School.pdf; 05 Compare Proj-Head Count 15.pdf

Meeting Feb 09, 2016 - Regular Meeting Category 8. INFORMATION Subject 8.1 2015 Enrollment Update Type Information DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION This report will provide the Board with an update of the October 2015 enrollment count and an analysis of trends observed. A summary of the District’s enrollment picture is as follows: General Fund Only Elementary down -19 (-.2%) Middle up 75 (1.2%) High up 204 (2.3%) Total K-12 up 260 (.9%) Total Enrollment (including Charters) Elementary down -12 (-.09%) Middle up 75 (1.05%) High up 222 (2.28%) Total K-12 up 285 (.95%) Not counting Preschool or students housed in outside facilities, overall District enrollment (general fund and charter) is up 285 students in 2015, representing an increase of .95% over last year. This gain in enrollment is under last year’s increase of 1.17%, and is slightly below the previous 5-year average of 1.1%. Of the total district gain in enrollment this year, growth was concentrated heavily in the general fund schools. With a gain of 260 students, or .9%, general fund growth was lower than the 393 student gain last year. Charter growth has generally slowed in recent years as space at these facilities has become more limited, with enrollment in 2015 increasing by only 25 students. General fund elementary growth posted its first decline (although slight) in 12 years decreasing by 19 students, or -.2%. Although negative, this change is on par with the previous 3 years which were only slightly positive, and signifies leveling growth at elementary. Previous to those years, the elementary level saw more robust growth between .6% and 2.5%. The prospects for future growth at this level seems to have narrowed as the last of the smaller cohorts (i.e. classes) from the early 2000’s have progressed on to the secondary level. Charter elementary enrollment had only a very slight gain (+7) in 2015. This year saw the smallest kindergarten class since 2004 at 1,966 students although it was only slightly smaller than last year’s class of 1,969. Kindergarten class size has been trending downward since its peak in 2011 of 2,113 students and the 2015 class is only the second in recent years to dip below 2,000 students. When looking at resident kindergarteners only, the current class shows a slightly greater decline from 1,869 in 2014 to 1,855 in 2015. Middle school general fund enrollment experienced a gain of 75 students, or 1.2% this year. This gain was largely due to moderate in-migration of new resident students, primarily at the 6 th grade level . This in-migration includes both new move-ins to the district and students previously enrolled in private or home schools. However, even with the gain this year, middle level enrollment growth is down from 2013 and 2014 (219 and 189 respectively) and will likely continue to level off now that smaller classes have progressed on to high school. Charter enrollment at the middle school level was level (+0) from last year. General fund high school enrollment posted strong gains in 2015, growing by 2.3%, or 204 students. These gains were primarily due to a small outgoing 12 th grade class coupled with a larger incoming 9 th grade class. The number of new out-of-district high school students was average for 2015, particularly at 9 th grade. Unlike the other grade levels, high school still retains stronger growth potential over the next two years as its relatively small 11 th grade class is replaced by larger in-coming 9 th grade classes progressing from lower grades. Charter high school enrollment grew slightly (+18) over last year’s level. Summary Overall enrollment growth was positive in 2015 gaining 285 students, or .95% over last year, primarily in the general fund schools. This was under last year’s gain of 346 (1.17%) but very close to the previous 5 year average of 1.1%. The bulk of overall enrollment growth for 2015 came with the departure of a relatively small 12 th grade class being replaced by larger classes coming up from lower grade levels. Still positive “in-migration” of new resident students to the District, who either physically moving to the District or enroll from previous home or private schooling also bolstered growth. New Out-of-district students entering BVSD was average for 2015. High school also continues to maintain high retention of resident students when compared to historical averages. Despite these gains, the District’s resident kindergarten classes have been in decline in recent years, especially among the resident student population. Although this would appear to be cause for concern, subsequent 1 st grade classes have not experienced a similar decline, although the 2015 first grade class was slightly smaller than average. One explanation for this may be that more parents are retaining their children in non-district early childhood education facilities through kindergarten and entering BVSD at 1 st grade. Included with this memorandum are a number of enrollment spreadsheets. Staff will also present a brief power point presentation. §

INFORMATION Subject 6.2 Update of the October 2016 Enrollment Count and an Analysis of Trends Observed Type Information DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION A summary of the District’s enrollment picture is as follows: General Fund Only Elementary down -17 (-.2%) Middle level 1 (0%) High down -43 (.5%) Total K-12 down -59 (.2%) Total Enrollment (including Charters) Elementary down -28 (-.21%) Middle level 7 (.1%) High down -43 (.-43%) Total K-12 down -64 (-.21%) Not counting Preschool or students housed in outside facilities, overall District enrollment (general fund and charter) is down 64 students in 2016, representing negative growth of .21% from last year. Enrollment as of October 5, 2016 stands at 30,141. This loss in enrollment is significantly under last year’s increase of 1%, and is well below the previous 5-year average of 1.05%. Of the total district change in enrollment this year, loss was concentrated almost entirely in the general fund schools. With a loss of 59 students, or .2%, general fund enrollment change was much lower than the +260 student gain last year. Charter growth has been level in recent years as space at these facilities has become limited, with enrollment in 2016 showing only a very slight loss (but well within normal variation) of 5 students. General fund elementary change posted a second year of decline, albeit small, decreasing by 17 students, or -.1%. Last year’s negative posting had ended a 12 year growth trend. Although negative, this change is on par with the previous 3 years which were only slightly positive, and signifies leveling and possibly declines at the elementary level. Previous to those years, the elementary level saw more robust growth between .6% and 2.5%. The prospects for future growth at this level seems to have narrowed as the last of the smaller cohorts (the population at each grade) from the early 2000’s have progressed on to the high school level. Charter elementary enrollment had a slight loss (-11) in 2016. This year saw the smallest kindergarten class since 2004 at 1,952 students although it was only a little smaller than the classes from the previous two years (1,966 and 1,969). Kindergarten class size has been trending downward since a peak of 2,113 students in 2011, but may be leveling now. The resident kindergartener class in particular was virtually unchanged from 2015. Middle school general fund enrollment experienced level growth in students or 0% this year. Middle school growth is similarly limited to elementary in that smaller cohorts have progressed onto high school. As is the case with elementary, this level is increasingly reliant on positive in-migration of new resident students, primarily at the 6 th grade level to experience growth . “In-migration” for the purposes of this analysis includes both new move-ins to the district and students previously enrolled in private or home schools. Charter enrollment at the middle school level was slightly bigger (+6) than last year. General fund high school enrollment posted the highest losses of all grade levels in 2016, falling by 43 students, or -.5%. This contrasts with growth last year of 2.3%, or 204 students. These losses were primarily due to a very large outgoing 12 th grade class coupled with underperforming in-migration in 2016. The number of new out-of-district (open enrolled) high school students was also below average for 2016, particularly at 9 th grade, which saw 20 fewer students from last year. Despite this, high school is likely to experience moderate to strong growth in 2017-18 as it progresses out a very small 12 th grade class and replaces it with a relatively strong 9 th grade. In the long-term, however, high school will likely also see more limited growth than in the past as this last small cohort graduates at the end of this school year. Conclusion Overall enrollment change was negative in 2016 losing 64 students, or .21% over last year, primarily in the general fund schools. This is well under the district’s recent level of growth near 1% and is the first year to year loss in 11 years. The bulk of the declining growth rate and overall enrollment loss for 2016 came from a combination of factors. A very large departing 12 th grade class put downward pressure on overall enrollment growth, especially at the high school level. In the past, when such a large class graduated, BVSD has made up the numbers through a combination of 9 th grade growth, out-of-district open enrollment, and in-migration of new students. However in 2016, BVSD did not see growth in these areas while in-migration of new resident students to the District was particularly below the level seen in recent years. Lower than average open enrollment filings from out-of-district students also contributed to the decline. Also noteworthy is the continued small kindergarten classes the District has been receiving the last three years. Kindergarten classes had been in decline, especially among the resident student population, but may be leveling off now. Staff has noted in previous analysis that this trend seems to be mitigated largely by near average 1 st grade class sizes, although 1 st grade also showed more decline in 2016. Overall, the large gap between kindergarten and 1 st grade may reflect that more parents are retaining their children in non-district early childhood education facilities through kindergarten and entering BVSD at 1 st grade. Included with this memorandum are a number of enrollment spreadsheets. Staff will also present a brief power point presentation. §

Charter enrollment experienced a small loss of -23 students, primarily from an enrollment decline at Boulder Preparatory High School. Otherwise, charter enrollment has been level as most available “seats” at these schools have been filled in recent years. General fund elementary enrollment posted a third year of decline, dropping by -172 students, or -1.4%. This level had experienced a 12 year growth trend with peak growth of 2.4% occurring in 2010. The prospects for future growth at this level have also narrowed as cohort size (enrollment at each grade) has leveled and started a gradual decline. As mentioned previously, declines at Boulder Preparatory High School lead to overall high school charter decline of -3.4% (-26) Conclusion Overall enrollment change was positive in 2017 gaining 141 students, or .5% over last year, primarily in the general fund schools. Growth in 2017 was fueled by a relatively small outgoing 12 th grade class, but was otherwise tempered by lackluster net migration to BVSD this year. In particular, high school net migration was negative for the first time since 2007 (-64). This net loss appears to come from a higher than usual number of high school students leaving prior to 12 th grade, although initial assessments show these losses are not due to increased drop-out. Also noteworthy are the continued small kindergarten classes the District has been receiving the last four years. Because of significant growth from kindergarten to 1 st grade over the same time period, staff has previously noted that these declines appear to be largely the result of more parents choosing other care options for Kindergarten-aged students. However, even with the increases at 1 st grade, the accumulating effect of these smaller 1 st grade classes are beginning to drag on overall enrollment. ## 2020-08-25 — 6.3 Enrollment Update  (item BSPFVP40FD2C; Regular Meeting)

Attachments: 8-21-20 Count Trend Funnel.pdf; Enrollment Update - 08-25-20.pdf

Meeting Aug 25, 2020 - Regular Meeting Category 6. INFORMATION Subject 6.3 Enrollment Update Type Information DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION Starting in August, when school staff are back in buildings processing student enrollments and parents are beginning to think about school starting, district staff closely monitor enrollment patterns and processing to ensure enrollment projections are on target and to provide as much time as possible to make adjustments in the event enrollment is coming in greater than or less than those expected targets. Staffing adjustments are always made prior to the start of the school year, and even into the first months of the school year as new students arrive or move out. The patterns of enrollment have been very consistent over the prior four years for all grade levels, with enrollments growing during early August, tapering off in late August when school starts and some students who are enrolled do not show up and are unenrolled, and then remains fairly flat until the final October Count numbers as the end data point. The attached report - "8-21-20 Count Trend Funnel" - shows this pattern. This year is significantly different, with the noticeable starting shortfall at the elementary level, which has continued to decline since the beginning of August. This is largely at the kindergarten grade, but is manifesting across all grades. There are some schools coming in at projected enrollment, but a small number overall. Most schools are below projections. There has also been a significant shift at all levels to Boulder Universal, BVSD's online school: Elementary Budgeted Enrollment - 2 08/21 Enrollment - 364 Middle Budgeted Enrollment - 17 08/21 Enrollment - 329 High Budgeted Enrollment - 51 08/21 Enrollment - 303 The current projection of where enrollment will land for a final October Count number is -692 below the initial projection for traditional schools (non-charter) which incorporated an expected loss of almost 200 students. There are obvious budget and staffing implications for such a significant, sudden and late shortfall in enrollment, although staffing reserves (unallocated positions) will address a significant portion of this shortfall, as well as the funding process of averaging student enrollment over 5 years for districts experiencing enrollment loss. It should be noted that this pattern of sudden and late enrollment drops is being experienced across the front range as districts are opening in different structures of online, at-home and in-person learning. §

INFORMATION Subject 6.3 October 2020 Enrollment Count and an Analysis of Trends Observed Type Information DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION This report will provide the Board with an update of the October 2020 enrollment count and an analysis of trends observed. A summary of the District’s enrollment is as follows: General Fund Only Elementary down -1,193 (-10.1%) Middle down -430 (-6.8%) High up 34 (0.4%) Total K-12 down -1,589 (-5.8%) Total Enrollment (including Charters) Elementary down -1,194 (-9.6%) Middle down -441 (-6.5%) High up 19 (0.2%) Total K-12 down -1,616 (-5.4%) Not counting preschool or students housed in outside facilities, overall district enrollment (general fund and charter) is down -1,616 students in 2020, representing a decline of -5.4% from last year. Enrollment as of October 1, 2020, stands at 28,366. This year’s loss represents a significant departure of loss rates below 1% for the previous 4 years. Last year’s loss of -219 students, or -0.73%, was the biggest drop in over 20 years up to that point. 2020 losses are largely a result of the current pandemic and appear to be due to increased numbers of parents choosing options outside BVSD rather than any fundamental demographic shift, although the local job market has some experienced disruption as well. However, even before the pandemic, BVSD was trending towards negative growth and the planning office did expect a drop in enrollment this year of -226 students. Enrollment decline in 2020 was heavily concentrated in the general fund schools which posted a loss of -5.8% (-1,589). Charter enrollment change was essentially flat in 2020 experiencing only a slight loss of 28 students, well within their typical year-to-year variation. General fund elementary enrollment posted a sixth year of decline, dropping by -1,193 students, or -10.1%. This grade level has been the most heavily impacted under pandemic conditions, but otherwise had been experiencing more modest declining growth in recent years. Overall, the prospects for future growth at this level have also narrowed for the foreseeable future as cohort size (enrollment at each grade) has continued to decline. In particular, entering kindergarten classes and, to a lesser extent, 1 st grade classes have been progressively smaller in recent years. However, enrollment losses in 2020 related to the pandemic far exceeded losses of -90 students (-0.85%) expected at this level under typical conditions. Charter enrollment at elementary remains flat, consistent with recent years. Kindergarten enrollment in 2020 was impacted especially hard by pandemic conditions, declining 20% over last year, with a loss of -371 students. It appears that parents are holding eligible kindergartners back a year at a higher rate than previous years, in addition to the same pressures of school choice experienced at other grades. However, along with elementary, kindergarten class size had been trending downward since a peak of 2,113 students in 2011 apart from a small enrollment increase in 2019 with the start of universal full day kindergarten. Losses at kindergarten had otherwise been expected to be relatively small at around -26 students, or -1.4% prior to the pandemic. Middle school general fund enrollment also experienced significant impacts related to the current pandemic conditions, declining by -430 students, or 7%. This level was already expected to decline in 2020 (-166/-2.4%) at a higher level than in recent years due to a large outgoing 8 th grade class. Overall, this grade level has been experiencing a leveling in class sizes similar to elementary, and is expected to continue declines in the coming years. Charter enrollment at middle school was only slightly below (-11) last year’s enrollment. General fund high school enrollment posted the only gains of all grade levels in 2020 increasing by 19 students, or 0.2% over last year. Enrollment at this grade level appears to be relatively unaffected by pandemic conditions and is very close to pre-pandemic expectations. This gain, however, is modest when compared to more recent years’ and high school enrollment does appear to be leveling as smaller middle school cohorts begin entering this level. High school charter enrollment was flat in 2020 with only -16 fewer students than the previous year. Overall, the conditions of school choice and disruption to the job market brought about by the COVID-19 pandemic have resulted in a significant drop in BVSD enrollment for 2020. Many other school districts in Colorado and the nation have experienced similar enrollment declines. For BVSD, the pandemic has been coupled with overall enrollment trend of negative growth, and a pre-pandemic expected loss of -200 students over 2019. This contrasts with generally positive growth over the last 15 years with 2020 being only the fourth year to post a loss in that time frame. Enrollment declines in 2020 were primarily concentrated at elementary with kindergarten seeing especially large losses. Middle school also experienced significant losses, but high school and charter enrollment levels were consistent with expectations and appeared to be unaffected by the pandemic. Enrollment as of October 1, 2021 stands at 28,087. This year’s decline was larger than expected with the district intending to recapture a greater portion of last year’s very large pandemic-related enrollment loss of -1,616 students. However, even before the pandemic, the district had started to slide into a more modest trend of enrollment decline beginning in 2016 which is expected to continue for a number of years. With the very large loss in 2020, the district may still experience short-lived gains in the next year or two as the district potentially recoups some of these students. Enrollment decline in 2021 was almost exclusively concentrated in the general fund schools which posted a loss of -1.1% (-278). Overall, however, the elementary level is expected to see future enrollment losses as cohort size (enrollment at each grade) continues to decline. In particular, entering kindergarten classes and, to a lesser extent, 1st-grade classes have been progressively smaller in recent years. At 1,729, the 2021 kindergarten enrollment showed progress in recouping last year’s losses while posting a positive +13.5% gain, but was otherwise still the second-smallest kindergarten class in decades, behind only last year’s extremely low number. By comparison, Kindergarten enrollment peaked in 2011 at 2,113 students. It appears that kindergarten enrollment is still impacted by concerns over the COVID-19 with some parents, albeit fewer than last year, likely preferring private or home school options, or delaying enrollment. Elementary charter enrollment was level (+3) in 2021. Middle school general fund enrollment saw the greatest losses of the three grade levels in 2021, declining by -255 students, or -4.3% from last year. Enrollment at this level also experienced significant impacts in 2020 related to the pandemic and may be struggling more than other levels to recoup lost students. Similar to elementary, middle school enrollment had also been leveling and starting to decline prior to the pandemic, which is a trend expected to continue in coming years. Charter enrollment at middle school was on par (-5) with last year’s enrollment. General fund high school enrollment posted its third loss in 10 years declining by -69 students, or -0.7% from last year. Enrollment at high school appears to have been relatively unaffected by pandemic conditions and is very close to pre-pandemic expectations. Overall, high school enrollment appears to be settling into a leveling trend as some of its larger classes (10 th and 12 th grade) progress out of the system. High school charter enrollment was flat in 2021. Conclusion Overall, BVSD enrollment saw declines again in 2021, but not nearly to the same degree as the pandemic conditions present in the fall of 2020. At that time, significant numbers of parents withheld or withdrew students from the system for program options outside BVSD, including home and private schools. This overall pattern was also experienced by school districts all over the country since 2020. Furthermore, BVSD had been sliding towards a future trend of declining enrollment in recent years, which further contributed to losses in the last two years. Enrollment declines in 2021 were primarily concentrated at the middle school level (-4.3%), differing significantly from 2020 where elementary, and especially kindergarten, saw the steepest declines. Although elementary grew modestly (+0.4%) in 2021, along with robust gains at kindergarten (+13.5%), these gains likely had more to do with recouping losses from 2020. Elementary is expected to continue its more modest downward trend once pandemic conditions stabilize in the coming years. High school and charter enrollment appear to be largely unaffected by the pandemic again in 2021 with high school experiencing a slight decline this year. Included with this memorandum are a number of enrollment spreadsheets. Staff will also present a brief slide show. §

Meeting Dec 13, 2022 - Regular Meeting Category 6. INFORMATION Subject 6.3 Enrollment Update and Trends Type Information, Presentation DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION This report will provide the Board with an update of the October 2022 enrollment count and an analysis of trends observed. A summary of the District's enrollment is as follows: General Fund Only Elementary down -245 (-2.3%) Middle down -195 (-3.5%) High down -157 (-1.7%) Total K-12 down -597 (-2.3%) Total Enrollment (including Charters) Elementary down -251 (-2.2%) Middle down -186 (-2.9%) High down -128 (-1.2%) Total K-12 down -565 (-2.0%) Not counting preschool or students housed in outside facilities, overall district enrollment (general fund and charter) is down -565 students in 2022, representing a decline of -2% from last year. Enrollment as of October 3, 2022, stands at 27,522. This year’s decline was larger than expected and it still appears the district is grappling with lingering COVID-19 impacts and uncertainty. However, even before the pandemic, the district had started to slide into a more modest trend of enrollment decline beginning in 2016 which is expected to continue for the foreseeable future. Enrollment decline in 2022 was almost exclusively concentrated in the general fund schools which posted a loss of -2.3% (-597). Charter enrollment change was essentially flat in 2022, which has generally been the case for previous years as well. General fund elementary enrollment saw a significant decline in enrollment in 2022, posting a loss of -245 students over last year, or -2.3%. This is in contrast to last year, where elementary enrollment gained slightly and appeared to capture students previously lost during pandemic-relate enrollment declines. The loss in 2022 was especially acute at kindergarten and 1st grade, both of which showed much weaker growth when compared to historic patterns, apart from 2020. Overall, the elementary level is expected to see future enrollment losses as cohort size (enrollment at each grade) continues to decline. Kindergarten enrollment in 2022 fell by -71 students over last year’s enrollment to 1,658 students. Apart from 2020, this is the lowest level for kindergarten in over 20 years. By comparison, kindergarten enrollment peaked in 2011 at 2,113 students. Although birth rates are in decline, BVSD appeared to capture fewer kindergarteners than in previous years, either due to families selecting different program options for young children (possibly still a lingering impact of the pandemic) or due to net outmigration among this group. Middle school general fund enrollment also saw significant losses in 2022, declining by -195 students, or -3.5% from last year. Although still a significant loss, middle-level declines were close to expectation and fared better than in 2021 where losses approached -4.3%. Similar to elementary, middle school enrollment had also been leveling and starting to decline prior to the pandemic, which is a trend expected to continue in coming years. General fund high school enrollment posted its second consecutive loss and most significant enrollment loss in over 20 years declining by -157 students, or -1.7% from last year. Enrollment at high school appeared to have been relatively unaffected by the pandemic but is now expected to start experiencing year-over-year declines as larger graduating classes are overtaken by smaller upcoming middle school classes. Conclusion Overall, BVSD enrollment saw declines at all levels in 2022. To some degree, these losses were expected and are a function of smaller class sizes progressing up from elementary and middle school. However, declines in 2022 were greater than expected, particularly at kindergarten and 1st grade. Declining birth rates do explain some of the loss at these early levels, however, this year’s kindergarten is smaller than what would have been expected based on birth rates alone. In addition, the 1st grade class in 2022 grew by significantly fewer students over last year than typically seen in pre-pandemic years. Included with this memorandum are a number of enrollment spreadsheets. Staff will also present a brief slide show. §
