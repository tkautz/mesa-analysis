Enrollment Big Picture
BVSD has been anticipating a gradual decline in enrollment in future years.
COVID-related enrollment loss accelerated this decline dramatically. Declining
numbers of students can lead to budget reductions, inefficient facility operations
and most importantly, a less comprehensive educational experience for
students at schools with smaller populations.

Long Range Advisory Committee 11
District and School Funding
● The School Finance Act funding formula approved by the legislature
determines how much funding each district is allocated based on the
formula and student counts.
○ Referred to as “Total Program Funding”
● Local property values determine how much of the funding is generated
locally via property tax and how much is provided through state resources.
○ BVSD is 84% local and 16% state funding in the finance act.
● Rising property values do not increase total resources for a school district.
● Declining student counts do not lower property tax bills.
● The district allocates resources to schools based on size, level, programs,
student needs and demographics within the total funding available.

Long Range Advisory Committee 14
Financial Constraints
● Each 1% drop in enrollment (300 students) = ~$3M reduction and 12
fewer teacher FTE via the standard staffing formulas.
● As enrollment declines at a school, fixed costs are distributed over
fewer students, increasing inefficiencies and the cost per student.
● Disproportionate resources are required to maintain smaller schools,
diverting funding from other student and district needs.
● As the number of smaller schools increases, the proportion of the
general fund allocated to subsidize those schools grows.
● The school district’s budget is about trade-offs; money spent in one
area cannot be spent elsewhere.

Long Range Advisory Committee 21
Metrics and Recommendations
Annual Report
Beginning in February of 2024, staff will provide an annual update to the
Board of Education regarding school utilization which also includes number of
classes per grade level and five-year enrollment projections by school.
Long Range Advisory Committee 22
Metrics and Recommendations
IF the annual report indicates that an elementary school has fewer than two classes
per grade level and enrollment is less than 60% of capacity;
THEN: Tier 1 - Small School Designation
The elementary school operates under the following conditions:
● Resource allocation will adhere to the established staffing funding formula to the
greatest extent possible
● Multi-age classrooms may be necessary to meet the funding formula
● Library, counseling, and specials may be limited
Long Range Advisory Committee 23
Metrics and Recommendations
IF an elementary school’s enrollment continues to decline below 50% capacity and fewer than 1.5
average classes per grade level with a projected trend to continue for at least 5 years;
THEN: Tier 2 - Community Engagement Process
The Board will engage staff to lead a community-based process to develop possible solutions to
provide a thriving, well-resourced educational environment for students and staff. Decision-making
should be in the context of looking at geographic areas or multiple schools if appropriate. Timeline will
be established by the Board.
This process may result in recommendations that could include but are not limited to:
● Continuing with “small school” status (i.e. Tier 1 operating conditions)
● Focus or specialized programming
● Reallocate grade levels with neighboring school
● Changes to open enrollment preferences
● Consolidate with adjacent school(s)

Guiding Principles
● Equity and access should be at the heart of every recommendation or decision in the enrollment
context. Consideration should be given regarding impacts to historically underserved
populations.
● Data should play a significant role in the decision-making process.
● Due to their unique geographic circumstances, mountain schools require differentiated
decisions.
● Decisions should aim to maximize efficient use of resources for students across the district.
● It should be understood that change is hard, and communities will need to be supported through
any transition.
● Municipalities should be included in conversations about closing or consolidating schools.
● The facility condition and the cost to modernize an existing school to meet educational
specifications should be taken into consideration.
