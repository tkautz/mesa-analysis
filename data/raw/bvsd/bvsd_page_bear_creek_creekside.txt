9/2/26, 10:12 PM Bear Creek/Creekside - Boulder Valley School District
Adopted Boundary Changes for the Bear Creek
Elementary and Creekside Elementary Dual Enrollment
Area
As part of the BVSD Board of Education’s regular review of attendance area boundaries, it adopted an
attendance boundary adjustment to the Bear Creek Elementary and Creekside Elementary attendance
areas at its Sept. 23, 2025 meeting (https://www.youtube.com/live/yfEsNIly9pQ?
si=sE3jMTgFg9dxmcqb&t=3523).
This change was proposed during the Board of Education’s review of attendance area boundaries this
spring, with discussions taking place during work sessions on March 11
(https://go.boarddocs.com/co/bvsd/Board.nsf/goto?open&id=DEAUVH7DE6AF), May 20
(https://go.boarddocs.com/co/bvsd/Board.nsf/goto?open&id=DF3M2M592725), and Sept. 9, 2025
(https://go.boarddocs.com/co/bvsd/Board.nsf/goto?open&id=DL5GY6460A53).
DETAILS
The change converts the Bear Creek/Creekside dual attendance area west of Broadway and north of
Table Mesa to Bear Creek only. All addresses in this area are within walking distance to Bear Creek and
will not receive school bus service.
The change will be effective beginning in the 2026-27 school year.

WHY MAKE THIS CHANGE?
This proposal is guided by several core principles, including:
Fostering community and neighborhood cohesion
BVSD believes there is value in supporting neighborhood-based relationships as students move
through the school grades. Assigning all students in this area to Bear Creek supports
neighborhood social connections.
Aligning with geographic features such as major roads
Broadway is a logical boundary and this change eliminates the necessity to cross Broadway to
access Creekside.
Maximization of pedestrian access to schools, and efficient use of transportation
The entire area is within walking distance of Bear Creek.
Balancing enrollment among schools in order to maintain high-quality learning experiences for
students
BVSD is committed to ensuring every student has access to high-quality learning experiences
and opportunities. When enrollment is uneven across schools, it impacts funding and the ability
to provide essential programs and staff, such as classroom teachers, counselors, and
specialists in art, music, P.E., and libraries. Fewer students can mean fewer adults to support
them, which affects the overall student experience.
