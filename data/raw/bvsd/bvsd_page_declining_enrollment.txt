The proposal
The plan proposes the closure of Mesa Elementary and merging of the school
communities in the Bear Creek building. The attendance area boundaries for both
schools would be redrawn to create a single attendance area, with addresses in the
consolidated area now assigned to the Bear Creek building.
Maps showing proposed boundary changes for Mesa
How we arrived at this proposal
While many schools in the South Boulder area have been impacted by declining
enrollment, Mesa is currently one of the lowest-enrolled schools, and projections show
its numbers are expected to continue to decline. Additionally, Mesa, which currently
uses about half of its capacity, also has the second-lowest number of resident students
in its attendance area.
Bear Creek, located less than a mile away, has a larger building capacity and can house
the projected enrollment of both schools in 2027-28 and beyond.
Closing Mesa reduces excess capacity in South Boulder. Combining the attendance
areas provides the resident student population for a three-round school at Bear Creek.
Bringing more students into Bear Creek will help grow and sustain the staffing,
programs, and opportunities that create a high-quality student experience.

The proposal
The plan proposes the closure of Flatirons Elementary at the end of the 2026-27 school year
and that its attendance area be divided between Foothill and Whittier Elementary Schools.
Addresses north of Boulder Canyon Drive would be assigned to Foothill Elementary, while
addresses south of Boulder Canyon Drive would be assigned to Whittier Elementary.
Maps showing proposed boundary changes for Flatirons
How we arrived at this proposal
Flatirons has the lowest enrollment of the Boulder schools, sustaining only a single class at
each level and using less than half of its building’s capacity. Closing the school reduces excess
capacity in Boulder. Whittier and Foothill each have the capacity to house a portion of Flatirons’
students.
Additional considerations
The RISE program currently at Flatirons would move to Whittier Elementary.
To help facilitate mountain routes, Whittier Elementary’s bell schedule will need to
shift 45 minutes later. Currently, it is 7:50 a.m. to 2:50 p.m. After the changes, it
would be 8:35 a.m. to 3:35 p.m., which aligns with other elementary schools that
serve mountain addresses.

The current Monarch and Eldorado middle-level attendance areas would combine into
one attendance area for both the middle and high schools. Additionally, Monarch PK-8’s
elementary attendance area would be divided among Fireside, Superior and Eldorado’s
attendance areas.
Maps showing proposed boundary changes for Louisville and Superior
How we arrived at this proposal
Following the engagement with our community and an analysis of school enrollment
and choice, it was evident that the Louisville/Superior communities needed a unique
plan of action focused on natural feeder areas/neighborhoods, while ensuring that
schools in this area are made more sustainable for the future.
Consolidating the middle grades on the Monarch campus creates the opportunity for
more robust middle school programming, as well as synergies with Monarch High
School.
Currently, fewer than half of Monarch’s K-5 resident students attend Monarch and
almost one third choose to attend Fireside, Superior or Eldorado. At the middle level,
more Eldorado resident 6-8 grade students choose to attend Monarch than Eldorado.
The proposed move reduces excess elementary capacity and increases resident
students for the receiving elementary schools and Monarch 6-8. Additional student
enrollment will help grow and sustain the staffing, programs, and opportunities that
create a high-quality student experience.

Dual Language Schools
During the process of reviewing BVSD's schools, the team identified some
opportunities for improvement with the dual language programs in both
Boulder and Lafayette. The team also recognized that more information is
needed before proceeding with a recommendation.
Review, Community Engagement & Return with a

Recommendation
The team’s proposed next steps are to take this next year to engage in a
thorough review process of BVSD’s PK-5 and 6-8 dual language models
in both Boulder and Lafayette. The review will include an internal analysis
of programming, a systematic review of national research, and
opportunities for teachers, families, and students to provide feedback.
The team will return to the Board at a yet to be determined time to
provide an update and recommendations, which could include program
and/or facility changes. Dual language programming remains a district
priority.

The closure and consolidation of schools is a significant change that affects students, families,
employees, and the broader community. While these decisions are difficult, we want to

Early efforts to address declining enrollment

The pace and scope of this work reflect sustained Board of Education interest and direction over

Fall 2025: Board directs staff to create proposal
Facing accelerating enrollment decline and the associated reduction of funding, the Board of
Education has determined that more substantial action is needed to allow BVSD to use
resources more efficiently in order to maintain the high-quality learning experiences students
currently receive.
School adjustment options the Board could consider include:
Introducing or expanding focus programs
Reconfiguring grades, e.g. K-2 & 3-5 or convert K-8s to elementary and middle schools
Relocating focus programs
