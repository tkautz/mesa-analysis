We are honored to share this final report of Boulder Valley School District’s

The Boulder Valley Board of Education created the Citizens’ Bond Oversight
Committee to provide a high level of accountability and transparency in
the implementation of the District’s Educational Facilities Master Plan.

budgets. As part of its responsibility, the group reviewed and made

earned through bond sales. In this role, the group is proud to have supported
projects such as additional air conditioning; a new building for Halcyon; and a
larger cafeteria at University Hill Elementary, among others.
Throughout the program, the committee toured many of the projects
and got to see first hand how this work has transformed learning
environments. These visits illustrate how each student, teacher, and
staff member in the District has been touched in positive ways by this
work.
It is with great pleasure that we celebrate with you the success of
this program.

Building with our community
Community input and oversight was a consistent theme throughout the program. This involvement ensured the Educational
Facilities Master Plan and the implementation of the projects within it met the needs and expectations of users and taxpayers.
The Board-appointed Capital Improvement Planning Committee (CIPC) represented the demographics of BVSD’s community and
worked with staff over a 12-month period to identify and and prioritize capital improvement needs and make final recommendations
to the board in the Educational Facilities Master Plan. The plan detailed improvement projects for each school and facility, and
reflected public input collected through Community Values Meetings, an online survey, and telephone polling. Once voters approved
the bond measure, several members of the committee continued to stay involved and participated on the Board-appointed
Citizens’ Bond Oversight Committee (CBOC). The committee met approximately every other month and, along with staff, presented
quarterly reports to the Board. The committee provided independent review of the projects in the Bond Program and reviewed and
recommended any significant changes in budget or scope. The group also provided recommendations to the Board for expenditures
of Bond premium funds.
Community voice was also included at the school level through design advisory teams (DAT) that included the principal, teachers,
staff members, parents, and sometimes community members and students. BVSD is grateful for the help of these groups, who
worked with the project team to help them understand the needs and perspectives of the building’s primary users. In cases where
project scopes shifted from the master plan, community stakeholders provided input regarding proposed changes. For example, the
Special Education Advisory Committee reviewed and provided feedback on the proposal to replace the Halcyon building, and the
New Vista Working Group recommended changing the scope for that project to a complete rebuild.

Innovation in action
The Educational Innovation component of the Building for Student Success program is a great example of what can be achieved
when a community supports their educators with the financial resources to do more with more.
Bond funding coupled with the roadmap of BVSD’s Strategic Plan, created an incredible opportunity to rethink the design of school
buildings and our approach to instruction. A portion of each school’s Bond project budget was allocated for making renovations to
learning environments to support innovative approaches to teaching and learning. In addition, the reconstruction of three schools
and the new construction of Meadowlark School created the opportunity to build innovative learning environments from the ground
up. The goal was to create spaces that will allow teachers to personalize learning for students, use multiple modes of delivering
instruction, and facilitate learning to fully engage students in their education.

Going further on sustainability
BVSD has made an organizational commitment to sustainability through policy and the Sustainability Management System (SMS).
Goals set in the SMS establish measurable benchmarks for efforts to protect student and staff health; conserve energy, water, and
other natural resources; reduce waste and pollution; and address climate change. BVSD made significant strides toward these goals
with the Building for Student Success Program, which further incorporated sustainability into the design and operation of BVSD
school buildings.
Sustainability was integrated into the program from the beginning of the planning process. Ambitious goals for waste diversion,
indoor air quality, and water and energy reduction were foundational in updating the Green Building Guiding Principles and technical
specifications, which served as guiding documents for project teams. Sustainability goals were tracked throughout the projects.
The principles require that new buildings meet 2009 LEED standards, although the district will not pursue certification. In addition,
where possible, all projects strove to use materials that “are durable, repairable, and reusable or recyclable; limit toxins and indoor
air pollutants; are made with high post-consumer recycled content; and are resource and energy efficient in their manufacturing,
use and disposal.”

from the 2008 baseline. This reduction represents decreased
emissions in areas of electricity, natural gas and transportation.

Prioritizing indoor air quality
The Building for Student Success program touched nearly every building in the District, creating the opportunity to comprehensively
impact indoor air quality (IAQ) across the District. BVSD’s Green Building Guiding Principles prioritized protecting IAQ, both during
construction and in finished projects. The guidelines called for using low VOC products such as paints, carpet, and furniture, along
with other measures to reduce the introduction of harmful substances into the indoor air.
Another significant way BVSD has been able to impact IAQ is through renovating HVAC systems and commissioning all projects,
which ensures that all systems are operating as they should. This work included installing centralized controls to program and
monitor ventilation centrally to ensure proper air flow. This capability was especially valuable during the Coronavirus pandemic.
Hand-in-hand with this work, in partnership with the University of Tulsa and the University of Colorado, BVSD launched an indoor air
quality initiative to collect IAQ data in schools. This data was used to understand current conditions and shape plans for renovations
and operations as well as occupant behavior. The data will also help BVSD understand the impact of Bond renovations on IAQ.
“BVSD is unique in having such a comprehensive approach to improving
indoor air quality. Through our efforts, the District will gain critical
insight into the “return on investment” to the classroom environment.”

Moving toward zero energy
BVSD’s 2013 Sustainable Energy Plan set ambitious goals for reducing energy use and controlling costs long term. These goals set
the stage for prioritizing energy efficiency in the 2014 Educational Facilities Master Plan. Community support of the Bond Program
enabled the district to take significant steps toward the long-term goal of being a zero-net energy district by 2050. How the District
builds and operates buildings has a tremendous impact on our carbon footprint. Building them right matters and will make a
difference for the long-term health of our planet and our students.
With BVSD’s ambitious energy goals in their sights, project designers were pushed to use more efficient equipment and strategies
which has yielded impressive results. The large scale of this work created the opportunity to use integrated design along with
commissioning for larger projects. The integrated design approach encourages collaboration among design professionals from the
beginning of the project. This team uses a whole building analysis to consider a building’s energy use holistically in all aspects of the
building’s design. The commissioning process confirms mechanical systems are operating as designed by reviewing programming
and ensuring equipment is calibrated correctly. All new buildings are now designed to be solar ready, and Bear Creek is ready for a
wind turbine. Most existing buildings received much-needed repair and upgrades for HVAC that improved energy efficiency as well
as performance, making buildings more comfortable. Other energy efficiency upgrades included LED lighting, particularly gyms;
new, more efficient boilers; replacing windows; replacing roofs and upgrading insulaiton; and installing pipe insulation.
Meadowlark School is one of the most energy efficient buildings in the district thanks to innovative technologies
such as the in-floor radiant heating and cooling system. This system was also used at the new Transportation
Center and is more comfortable for occupants because temperature is controlled on the floor where people are
working rather than in the air above them.

Expanding opportunities for our youngest learners
BVSD believes that strong educational opportunities in the early years set children on a path to academic success. Through the
Bond Program, BVSD expanded our capacity to provide a strong academic start for students across the district. New kindergarten
classrooms and playgrounds were built to accommodate full-day kindergarten programs. When full-day kindergarten funding
became a reality in Colorado, BVSD was prepared and ready to welcome students to fully-equipped learning spaces designed for
their needs. Similarly, BVSD added and renovated preschool rooms and playgrounds throughout the district to give more Boulder
Valley children access to high-quality preschool experiences. All of these learning spaces are filled with engaging, inspiring materials
that spark curiosity and the thrill of discovery in our youngest learners.

Special spaces for special programs
The Halcyon School houses the district’s day-treatment program which, gives students access to their education while also
providing therapeutic behavioral interventions and strategies. The Bond Program replaced most of a decades-old structure with
a nearly 15,000-square-foot new building that was intentionally designed with trauma-informed features to meet the needs of
the program and create a safe, comfortable space for students. The new building honors the historical architecture of the original
building and neighborhood, and features separate elementary and secondary wings along with administrative spaces, a cafeteria/
multi-purpose room, small group rooms, and outdoor recreation spaces. Special Education rooms in many other BVSD schools were
also renovated in a variety of ways to improve functionality and comfort for the students and staff that use them.

Building a better home for BVSD buses
Every school day, BVSD buses drive approximately 12,300 miles to take over 5,000 students to and from school. BVSD’s new
40,900-square-foot transportation terminal in Boulder provides a brand new home for hundreds of buses from BVSD’s fleet. The
new facility, which replaces a decades-old structure on the main Education Center campus, was moved to a larger site nearby to
reduce traffic congestion at the other location. At more than twice the size of the old building, the new facility provides three more
maintenance bays, significantly more storage, and room for driver training, something that was lacking in the old facility.
Boosting team member safety and efficiency
A number of features in the new facility will improve worker safety and comfort in the shop. Larger maintenance bays provide ample
space for mechanics to safely move around the vehicles, equipment, and parts. Multiple moveable lifts elevate vehicles, allowing
mechanics to work standing up vs. on the ground, reducing physical strain. Better lighting combined with light colored floors provide
better visibility. An in-floor radiant heating and cooling system provides heat/cooling where the people are instead of in the air above
them. The drive-through vehicle wash eliminates the risk of slipping on wet surfaces when washing by hand. Digital dashboards
throughout the facility will make tasks ranging from ordering parts to scheduling training and preventive maintenance easier.
Built green from the ground up
The new facility is predicted to be 57% more energy efficient than a code-compliant building of the same type. Features such as a
high-efficiency HVAC system, interior and exterior LED lighting, daylighting, and self-dimming parking lot lighting all contribute to
the impressive efficiency predictions. The facility supports BVSD’s commitment to sustainable transportation with both a propane
filling station and EV charging on site for passenger vehicles and buses.

A kitchen built to deliver freshness and quality
BVSD has worked for over a decade to transition to a scratch-
cooked food program built on the cornerstones of freshness and
local sourcing. Thanks to the belief of local voters that fresh food
matters, the district took a giant step forward in fulfilling that
mission with the construction of the new Culinary Center.
The new facility replaces three regional production kitchens that
were located at schools. The regional production kitchens were
never designed or sized for producing the amount of scratch-
cooked food they were charged with, but had been retrofitted to
get by over the years. While these kitchens served well to support
the district’s transition to scratch-cooked food, the retro-fitted
facilities presented challenges, particularly related to food prep
space and storage. The new facility can expand production up to
17,000 daily meals as demand grows.
A place for everything
The new 33,591-square-foot facility will reduce food and operating
costs while allowing the district to expand initiatives such as
nutrition education and addressing food insecurity. The Culinary
Center features a loading dock with a fresh food processing area
for deliveries, three food prep areas, abundant storage, a blast chill
area, and business spaces. Features such as precise temperature
control and separate areas for preparing raw meat and fresh
veggies promote food safety. Production of food at one location
provides more control over quality and waste.

The Education Center project, which included 45,000 square feet of renovation and 26,700 square feet of new construction,
reconfigured the district’s administration building to improve customer service to families and expand space for staff training.

Financial management
Bond debt to generate proceeds to fund projects was issued in three increments, in 2015, 2017, and 2019. In all cases, the bonds
were sold online in a competitive bid market. The bids received reflect the bond market’s very high level of confidence in the
district, which results in lower interest cost for taxpayers. Premium dollars were earned on each sale providing additional revenue to
the District, which was invested into projects.
2015
In April 2015, BVSD issued the first set of general obligation bonds in the amount of $250 million to fund the first phase of the Bond
Program. Premium earned on this sale generated an additional $30,812,900.
2017
In March 2017, the second round of general obligation bonds was sold. The total amount issued was $283,740,000, including
$190,000,000 issued for facilities improvements. $93,740,000 of the 2017 bonds were being issued to refinance the District’s
series 2007 general obligation bonds. The refinancing lowered the interest rate on this portion of the District’s debt, and will allow
it to be paid off sooner, producing a total debt service savings of more than $22 million. Premium earned on this sale generated an
additional $29,324,600.
2019
In January 2019, BVSD offered its final series of bonds in a competitive bid process. The sale had two components - a refunding of
$172.18M of 2009 debt and the final issuance of the 2014 authorization of $136.52 million. The refunding generated savings to
taxpayers of $33.0 million over the next 15 years, at a net present value savings of $26.4 million. Related to the final issuance of
voter-approved debt, the sale generated a premium of $20,867,275 million.
The total bond premium earned from all sales was $81,004,775.
Recommendations for the allocation of this additional revenue were made by the Citizens’ Bond Oversight Committee and approved
by the Board of Education. The allocations augmented existing project budgets to address additional scope or cost escalation. In
addition, premium dollars were allocated to Program Reserve and for a land purchase for the new Transportation Center.
Investment Earnings
Revenue earned from the investment of earnings totaled $12,825,000 and was invested back into projects.
Additional Revenue
In some cases, individual schools contributed funds toward projects. Overall the total contributions from schools was $1,803,840.
The district received rebates from Xcel in the amount of $878,000 for energy efficiency improvements. Additional contributions
also were provided by municipalities, totaling $4,653,665.
Overall, the total additional revenue provided to the Bond Program from these various sources totaled $100,287,279.
