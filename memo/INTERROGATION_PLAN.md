## B. The district's load-bearing claims, and how to test each

### B1. "Bear Creek can house the projected enrollment of both schools in 2027–28 and beyond" (bvsd.org page p. 7; deck p. 51)
Already tested (appendix §5–6). Two additions:
- **B1a [data in hand] Which receiving schools sit near capacity at the top of their range?** From the deck's own post-change tables (pp. 25, 37–39, 48, 51, 54): Superior 447–458 of 467 (96–98%), Foothill 484–492 of 565 (86–87%), Kohl 437–474 of 541 (81–88%), Bear Creek 403–462 of 492 (82–94%). Pushback: the district's own tables put two receiving schools within 10 seats of capacity at the top of their range before any upside case; "fits" is doing a lot of work across the package. (Half a day; one table.)
- **B1b [data in hand] Rounds arithmetic on the district's own range.** "Boosts enrollment at the receiving school to 3-rounds" (p. 50) holds only at the top of the range: 462/150 = 3.1 rounds; 403/150 = 2.7. Under the district's own numbers the merged school is a three-round school only if fewer than about a third of Mesa's students leave. (One paragraph.)

### B2. "Closing Mesa reduces excess capacity in South Boulder"; "capacity reduction 1,906" (page p. 7; deck p. 58)
- **B2a [data in hand] Decompose the Boulder utilization change.** Deck p. 43: Boulder now 63.1% enrolled, 2,585 open seats, 16 PK-5 schools, "10 needed to sustain three classes/grade", 3,522 projected residents and 4,241 projected enrollment in 2030; p. 55: 67.9% after all changes. Compute Mesa's share of the 1,906-seat reduction (418 = 22%) and the utilization gain from Mesa alone. Pushback: closing Mesa moves regional utilization by a few points; the district's own "10 schools needed" implies six Boulder closures and the package delivers three, so either the goal is not the goal or this is the first tranche. Ask which. (Half a day.)
- **B2b [data in hand] Capacity is a policy variable.** Bear Creek 467 → 492 (2024 → 2025); Meadowlark's four portables "not included in capacity" (Feb 2026 p. 9 footnote); Aspen Creek at 107–116%. Table the schools whose LRAC status flips if capacity moves ±5%. Pushback: the 60% threshold is applied to a denominator the district restates without explanation; a 5% restatement moves Bear Creek from 63% to 67% and moves several schools across the advisory line. Ask, do not assert. (Half a day; capacity tables already parsed.)

### B3. "Mesa is among the lowest enrolled and has the second-lowest number of resident students" (deck p. 50)
True as stated (p. 44: Flatirons 195, Mesa 228). Three tests of what it means:
- **B3a [data in hand + CORA] Capture, not residents, is Mesa's problem.** 228 residents, 156 attend (68%); 72 residents go elsewhere; 68 students choose in. Bear Creek: 275 residents, 217 attend (79%). Pushback: the district has never said where Mesa's 72 departing residents go; if to Bear Creek, the merger does nothing for them; if to focus schools, the merger does not bring them back. Needs the sending/receiving matrix (F.4).
- **B3b [data in hand] Mesa serves a higher-need population than Bear Creek.** 2025-26 special-programs file: free lunch 11.6% vs 9.0%; SPED 21.4% vs 15.4% (Mesa houses RISE); 504 plans 5.8% vs 2.9%; Hispanic 10.3% vs 8.0%; TAG 13.8% vs 24.0%. Pushback: the transition burden of this component falls disproportionately on students with IEPs and lower-income families, and the proposal's "Equitable Funding" theme (p. 17) and "maintain and improve learning outcomes for all students" outcome (p. 14) are not evaluated against that. Present as a table, no adjectives. (Two hours.)
- **B3c [data in hand] Mesa's decline is not what it was.** 2014–2020: −95; 2020–2025: −11; kindergarten 26–38 every year since 2016. Already in the appendix; promote to the ledger.

### B4. "The projections show continued decline" (deck pp. 11–13; Feb 2026 p. 6: 26,607 → 24,937, −1,670)
- **B4a [data in hand] Elementary-total revision history.** The three capacity tables carry elementary totals: Jan 2024 projected 9,174 for 2028-29; Jan 2025 projected 9,589 for 2028-29 and 9,431 for 2029-30; Jan 2026 projects 9,273 for 2028-29, 9,319 for 2029-30 and 9,408 for 2030-31. Chart the three total paths. Pushback: even at district level the four-year-out total moved by hundreds of students between runs, and the 2030-31 total rises in the latest run. (Two hours.)
- **B4b [fetch] Independent demographic check.** CDPHE births for Boulder County by year and the State Demography Office single-year-of-age projections. The district says births are "leveling" (news article p. 1) but publishes no series. With the data we can test the 93%-of-births claim, project kindergarten from births directly, and show what the district's own stated method implies. Most likely to move a planner. (One day once data is in hand.)
- **B4c [data in hand] The district's run sits on the level assumption for both schools.** Bear Creek 320 vs 317 (level) / 268 (trend); Mesa 201 vs 205 (level) / 180 (trend). Pushback: the district's own run is consistent with kindergarten having stopped falling at both schools, which is the case in which the merged school does not fit. (One hour.)

### B5. "Three-round schools give smaller classes and full-time specialists; this is only possible through consolidation" (deck pp. 8–9, 60–61; FAQ p. 3)
- **B5a [data in hand] The K-2 / 3-5 reconfiguration meets the district's own goal without closing a building.** Simulate the rejected option (deck p. 56): Mesa as K-2 and Bear Creek as 3-5 (or the reverse) for the combined area. Under the level assumption the combined area has roughly 260 in K-2 and 260 in 3-5 in 2030-31, about 85–90 per grade, three-plus classes per grade in each building at 60–65% utilization; under the trend assumption about 215 and 215. Pushback: the district's stated goal (three classes per grade, dedicated specialists) is reachable with both buildings open, every child within 0.71 miles of both, no irreversible step; the option was listed as studied, its fiscal impact called "uncertain" in October, and no comparison published. The strongest substantive alternative in the record, and it is the district's own. (One day, both specifications, with a class-size table.)
- **B5b [fetch] What $3.5–4.0M buys.** BVSD's adopted budget and staffing formula give teacher cost and per-school allocation. Compute how many teachers $3.5–4.0M funds across ~30 elementary schools and the implied class-size change (order of one student per class). Pushback: the district-wide benefit is real but small per classroom; this component's share is a fraction of one teacher per school. (Half a day once the budget book is in the repo.)
- **B5c [CORA] School-level budgets for Mesa and Bear Creek.** The only way to put a number on "keeping Mesa open is cheap" and on the district's "not fiscally efficient." See F.5.

### B6. "Combining the attendance areas provides the resident student population for a three-round school" (page p. 7; deck p. 51)
- **B6a [data in hand] The resident numbers do not reconcile** (503 → 473 → 522 on p. 51 vs about 445 on p. 44; already in the email). Add: at Bear Creek's own 79% capture, 522 residents yield 412 resident students, below three rounds, so the "three-round school" depends on the open enrollment the FAQ says will be managed. (One paragraph.)
- **B6b [data in hand] Geography of the combined area.** `data/raw/enrollmentdata/BVSD_Attendance_Areas.geojson` has the Mesa, Bear Creek and dual polygons. Compute the share of the Mesa polygon (area, and roughly students via the GIS `StdtPop` attribute) beyond 1.5 miles from Bear Creek (2500 Table Mesa Dr). Pushback: that share is entitled to transportation the deck does not cost. Label as area-based, not address-based. (Half a day.)

### B7. "Enrollment projections take into account managing choice enrollment beginning in 2027-28" (FAQ p. 3)
- **B7a [CORA] The open-enrollment matrix and the choice-management assumptions by receiving school.** See F.4 and F.1.
- **B7b [data in hand] What Heatherwood shows about announcement effects.** Heatherwood entered the community-engagement phase in 2023-24; counts 226, 233, 225 against projections 227 and 217. Honest reading: no visible exodus after designation. Use it to bound our own announcement-effect claim, and to say the same restraint applies to "staying small is not an option." (One hour.)

### B8. "Staying small is not fiscally efficient; under-enrolled schools carry significant, ongoing costs" (work session slide 45; FAQ pp. 1, 7)
- **B8a [CORA]** No number exists in the record. The pushback is the absence: an irreversible step is being justified by a cost the district has not quantified for the school in question, while package savings are quantified to the half-million.
- **B8b [data in hand] Per-school arithmetic.** $3.5–4.0M across six closed schools is $0.6–0.7M per school per year. Ask what a two-building South Boulder configuration costs relative to that, and note that B5a keeps both buildings while consolidating grade-level staffing. (One paragraph.)

### B9. "14 schools below two classes per grade → 6; utilization 68% → 75%" (deck p. 58)
- **B9a [data in hand] Recount from the deck's own ranges.** Using pp. 25, 37–39, 48, 51, 54 and Feb 2026 for unchanged schools, count post-change schools below two rounds at the bottom vs the top of their ranges. Pushback: the headline depends on the top of every range at once. (Half a day.)

### B10. "Boulder needs 10 schools to sustain three classes per grade" (deck p. 43)
- **B10a [data in hand] What the number implies.** 3,522 residents ÷ 450 = 7.8 schools; 4,241 enrollment ÷ 450 = 9.4. The package leaves 13. Pushback: by the district's own arithmetic this package is not the end state; the Board should know the next tranche before approving the first, and whether Bear Creek's three-round status survives it. (One paragraph.)
