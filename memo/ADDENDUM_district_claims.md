## 3. The district's rejected K-2 / 3-5 option meets its own "three classes per grade" goal in most cases (deck p. 56; `table07_reconfiguration.csv`, Figure 12)
Combined-area grade paths from the model, 90% of Mesa's students remaining in the area, 2030-31:

| Assumption | K-2 building | per grade | classes at 25 | 3-5 building | per grade | classes at 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Level | 231 (193–273) | 77 | 3.1 | 271 (226–323) | 90 | 3.6 |
| Trend | 183 (146–221) | 61 | 2.4 | 248 (205–295) | 83 | 3.3 |

In 2027-28: Level 234 / 260 (78 and 87 per grade; 3.1 and 3.5 classes); Trend 213 / 259 (71 and 86; 2.8 and 3.5). Utilization: Mesa's 418 seats at 231 = 55%; Bear Creek's 492 at 271 = 55% (level, 2030-31).
**What it shows.** Under the level assumption both buildings run three-plus classes per grade with both open, every child within 0.71 miles of both schools, and no irreversible step. Under the trend assumption the upper-grade building does; the K-2 building falls to about 2.4 classes per grade by 2030-31, because cohorts grow between kindergarten and fifth grade. **What it does not show:** cost. Two buildings cost more than one, and the district has not said by how much (item 8). The district listed this option as studied (p. 56); in October its fiscal impact was "uncertain" (work session slide 45); no comparison has been published.
**Recommend:** add to the appendix as a short section with Figure 12 and to the email's open questions ("what did the K-2/3-5 comparison show?"). Pair with item 8. Acknowledge the K-2 building's 2.4 under the trend assumption in the same breath.

## 5. Package-wide fit: Bear Creek has the widest range and, with Superior, the least margin (`table08_package_ranges.csv`; deck pp. 25, 37, 48, 51, 54)
Post-change 2030-31, top of range vs capacity: Bear Creek 462/492 (94%, 30 seats left, range width 59); Superior 436/467 (93%, 31 seats, width 12); Kohl 474/541 (88%); Coal Creek 427/492 (87%); Foothill 492/565 (87%); Fireside 430/516 (83%). Bear Creek's range is two to seven times wider than any other receiving school's, which is the deck's own statement that its Mesa retention assumption is the most uncertain input in the package. In 2027-28 Superior is at 96–98% of capacity.
**Recommend:** appendix §6 or §9, one paragraph and the table. Pushback line: the deck's own ranges say Bear Creek is the component whose fit depends most on an unstated assumption.

## 6. Recount of "14 → 6 schools below two classes per grade" (deck p. 58; `table08_unchanged_2030.csv`)
From the deck's post-change ranges plus the Feb 2026 projections for unchanged schools, schools projected below 300 in 2030-31 after the package: Heatherwood (260–268), Creekside (215), Community Montessori (167), Sanchez (298), and the three mountain schools; Eisenhower is below 300 at the low end of its range (297–302) and Whittier is within 30 (315–329). That is 7 including exempt mountain schools, 4 excluding them, or 5 at the low end. The deck says 6; its definition (which schools are exempt, which end of the range) is not stated.
**Recommend:** appendix, one sentence: the headline depends on definitions the deck does not give and on the top of each range. Do not lead with it.

## 7. "Boulder needs 10 schools to sustain three classes per grade" (deck p. 43; `table08_boulder_arithmetic.csv`)
3,522 projected residents ÷ 450 = 7.8 schools; 4,241 projected enrollment ÷ 450 = 9.4; the package leaves 13 PK-5 schools in Boulder. Mesa is 418 of the package's 1,906-seat capacity reduction (22%).
**Recommend:** appendix §9, two sentences; email open question: "By the deck's own arithmetic Boulder would still have 13 schools where 10 are said to be needed. Is this the first tranche, and does Bear Creek's three-round status survive the next?"

## 8. The cost the district has not shown (`table08_boulder_arithmetic.csv`; deck p. 60)
$3.5–4.0M recurring savings across six closed schools is $0.6–0.7M per school per year, package-wide. No Mesa figure exists. The K-2/3-5 option keeps both buildings but consolidates grade-level staffing, so its cost relative to closure is the number the Board needs and does not have.
**Recommend:** CORA request items 5 and 7 (interrogation plan §F) this week.

## 9. District-level totals are revised too (`table08_elementary_totals_by_run.csv`; capacity-table total rows)
Elementary total projected for 2028-29: 9,174 (Jan 2024 run), 9,444 (Jan 2025), 9,273 (Jan 2026): a swing of +3.0% then −1.8% at the district level. The Jan 2026 run's total rises from 9,273 (2028-29) to 9,408 (2030-31).

## 10. The district's run sits on the level assumption for Mesa as well (`table03_intervals.csv`)

**Recommend:** appendix §5.2, one sentence adding Mesa to the Bear Creek comparison.

## 11. Heatherwood: no visible exodus after designation (`bvsd_pupil_count_all_elementary.csv`)
Heatherwood entered the community-engagement phase in 2023-24; counts 226, 233, 225 against Jan 2024 projections of 227 and 217. **Recommend:** keep the "announcement effect" item in the appendix as unquantified, and do not argue it in the email.

## 12. Geography of the Mesa attendance area (`table09_geography.csv`; BVSD ArcGIS polygons)
About 89% of the Mesa attendance area's land area lies more than 1.5 miles (straight line) from Bear Creek, and the polygon's farthest point is 5.6 miles away; 82% of the area is already more than 1.5 miles from Mesa itself. This is area, not addresses: most residents live in the dense northern part near both schools. **What it shows:** "0.71 miles apart" describes the two buildings, not the attendance area; the transportation question is real for the outer part of the area. **Recommend:** appendix, one sentence with the caveat, in the transportation gap item. Do not use an address count you do not have; ask for it (CORA item 8).
