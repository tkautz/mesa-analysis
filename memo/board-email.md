**Subject:** Mesa/Bear Creek: enrollment evidence before the Sept. 22 vote

**To:** BVSD Board of Education (seven members)
**Cc:** Superintendent

Dear Board members,

I am the parent of a Mesa Elementary kindergartner and a PhD economist at Mathematica, writing personally.

The district's own January 2026 projections for Bear Creek and Mesa add to 521 students in 2030-31. The Bear Creek building holds 492. The proposal's range tops out at 462. The district's website says Bear Creek "can house the projected enrollment of both schools," but no document shows how the 522 residents it projects for the combined area become 403–462 in the building; at today's capture rate and open enrollment, 522 residents give about 550. The FAQ says each receiving school's "open enrollment, space and staffing plan" will be developed "during the transition year," after the vote. The Board is asked to accept a conclusion before seeing the arithmetic. I therefore ask the Board to decline the Mesa/Bear Creek component on September 22 and direct staff to return with the analysis described here.

I concede Mesa is small, has been on the enrollment-advisory list every year it has existed, and that the district's central projection is reasonable; my own model agrees. The question is whether the merged school fits the building.

**What the Board should have before closing a school:**
1. A projection with a stated margin of error, tested against the district's own October counts.
2. A one-page Bear Creek worksheet: projected residents, capture rate, Mesa-area students expected at Bear Creek, open-enrolled students kept, choice seats restricted, and classrooms for RISE, AIM and general education.
3. Proof the merged school fits Bear Creek under the higher cases, not just the middle one.
4. Mesa-specific one-time costs, recurring savings and revenue lost with departing families.
5. A transition plan for RISE, school-age care, preschool and transportation.

**How much the projections move** (appendix sections 3–4). Between the January 2025 and January 2026 runs, the district's 2029-30 projection for Bear Creek rose from 272 to 310 and Mesa's fell from 224 to 202. Across all BVSD elementary schools, one two-year projection in ten misses by over 13%. Among the ten receiving schools in the proposal, Bear Creek has the widest range (59 students; the next is 37) and the smallest margin at the top of it (30 seats).

**What the range assumes** (sections 5–6). The district's Bear Creek projection for 2030-31 is 320. An independent cohort-survival model patterned on the district's published method and tested on 26 schools reproduces it (317) only if kindergarten intake stops falling and holds at its 2023-25 average. Under that assumption the two schools together are about 523 and the chance the merged school exceeds the building is 57%; if intake keeps falling, the estimate is 430 and the chance is 16%. The documents do not say which assumption the district uses; the answers are 73 students apart, wider than the range. Under either, the chance of landing near three rounds and inside the building is about one in four. Read on residents, 403–462 requires either a capture rate of 46–57%, below either school's today, or cutting choice seats from 163 to between 16 and 75.

**Interdependence** (section 9). Every projection is fitted on flows observed under a portfolio that ends in 2027-28; the district has not modeled the effect on Bear Creek, and the October 2026 count could move the estimate by about two classrooms either way.

**Cost and benefit** (section 10). The deck states package savings of $3.5–4.0M a year against one-time costs of $7.5–10M plus $5.0M, and no Mesa-specific figure. The range assumes 29–59% of Mesa's projected students do not follow, and the deck does not say what funding follows them.

**Open questions** (section 11):
- How many classrooms will AIM and RISE occupy at Bear Creek, and does 492 net them out?
- Does the Bear Creek range assume choice management, and how many seats?
- Why do the deck's resident counts for the combined area read 503, 473 and 522 on p. 51 but about 445 on p. 44?
- Why was the K-2/3-5 reconfiguration, called "uncertain" in October, set aside without a published comparison?

**The ask.** Decline to approve the Mesa/Bear Creek component of the Resilient Schools proposal at this time, and direct staff to return with an analysis that meets the five points above. If staff can produce the worksheet and it shows a fit, the Board has a fair test.

The attached appendix is reproducible from public documents, with a page citation for every number; I would gladly walk any Board member or staff through it before Sept. 22.

Respectfully,

Tim Kautz
