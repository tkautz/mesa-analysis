**Subject:** Mesa/Bear Creek: enrollment evidence before the Sept. 22 vote

**To:** BVSD Board of Education (seven members)
**Cc:** Superintendent

Dear Board members,

I am the parent of a Mesa Elementary kindergartner and a PhD economist at Mathematica, writing personally; the views are my own.

The district's own January 2026 projections for Bear Creek and Mesa add to 521 students in 2030-31. The Bear Creek building holds 492. The proposal's range tops out at 462. The gap is an assumption about how many Mesa families leave, or how much open enrollment gets cut, and the proposal does not say which. For that and the reasons below, I ask the Board to decline the Mesa/Bear Creek component on September 22 and direct staff to return with the analysis described here.

I concede Mesa is small, has been on the enrollment-advisory list every year it has existed, and that the district's central projection is reasonable; my own model agrees. The question is whether the merged school fits the building, and there the evidence is thin.

**What the Board should have before closing a school:**
1. A projection with a stated margin of error, tested against the district's own October counts.
2. The assumptions behind the merged-school range: how many Mesa students follow, how many residents attend, how much open enrollment.
3. Proof the merged school fits Bear Creek under the higher cases, not just the middle one, net of RISE and AIM classrooms.
4. Mesa-specific one-time costs, recurring savings and revenue lost with departing families.
5. A transition plan for RISE, school-age care, preschool and transportation.

**How much the projections move** (appendix sections 3–4). Between the January 2025 and January 2026 runs, the district's 2029-30 projection for Bear Creek rose from 272 to 310 and Mesa's fell from 224 to 202; its 2028-29 Bear Creek figure went 248, 274, 288 in three runs. Across all BVSD elementary schools, one two-year projection in ten misses by over 13% (n = 29).

**What the range assumes** (sections 5–6). The district's Bear Creek projection for 2030-31 is 320. An independent model fitted to the district's own grade counts and tested on 26 schools reproduces it (317) only if kindergarten intake stops falling and holds at its post-2020 level. Under that assumption the two schools together are about 523, 403–462 is reachable only if some 60 to 120 students go elsewhere, and the chance the merged school exceeds the building is 57%. If intake keeps falling, the estimate is 430, inside the range, and the chance is 16%. The documents do not say which assumption the district uses; the answers are 73 students apart, wider than the range. Read on residents instead, the range requires either a capture rate below either school's today or open enrollment cut to near zero, and cutting choice pulls the school back toward two and a half rounds.

**Interdependence** (section 9). Every projection, the district's and mine, is fitted on flows observed under a portfolio that ends in 2027-28, when three schools close, two focus schools move, boundaries are redrawn and new choice priorities apply. The identifiable channels point toward more students at Bear Creek, one of very few Boulder-region schools near three rounds; the district has not modeled them. The October 2026 count, the first under the new boundaries, could move the estimate by about two classrooms either way.

**Cost and benefit** (section 10). The deck states recurring savings of $3.5–4.0M a year for the whole package against one-time costs of $7.5–10M for facilities plus $5.0M for transition, a three-to-four-year payback if realized, and no Mesa-specific figure. The range assumes 29–59% of Mesa's projected students do not follow; the deck does not say where they go or what funding follows them.

**Open questions** (section 11):
- How many classrooms will AIM and RISE occupy at Bear Creek, and does 492 net them out?
- What share following, capture rate and open enrollment does 403–462 assume?
- Why do the deck's resident counts for the combined area read 503, 473 and 522 on p. 51 but about 445 on p. 44?
- Where will preschool be offered? Locations are promised only by Oct. 1.
- Why was the K-2/3-5 reconfiguration, called "uncertain" in October, set aside without a published comparison?

**The ask.** Decline to approve the Mesa/Bear Creek component of the Resilient Schools proposal at this time, and direct staff to return with an analysis that meets the five points above.

The attached appendix is reproducible from public documents, with a page citation for every number; I would gladly walk any Board member or staff through it before Sept. 22.

Respectfully,

Tim Kautz
