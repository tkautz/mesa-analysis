**Subject:** Mesa/Bear Creek consolidation: the enrollment evidence does not yet support an irreversible decision

**To:** BVSD Board of Education (seven members)
**Cc:** Superintendent

Dear Board members,

I am the parent of a Mesa Elementary kindergartner. I am also a PhD economist and a senior researcher at Mathematica, writing in a personal capacity; the views are my own.

The district proposes an irreversible step on the basis of projections with no stated uncertainty. Its own projections for these two schools have moved 10–16% between annual runs, and one more October count could shift the merged school's expected enrollment by two classrooms in either direction. Keeping Mesa open is cheap and reversible; closing it is not.

I concede Mesa is small, has been on the enrollment-advisory list every year it has existed, and that the district's central projection is not unreasonable. My concern is the evidence bar for an irreversible step.

**The standard.** Before approving any closure, the Board should have (i) a projection with stated prediction intervals, back-tested against the district's own October counts; (ii) the retention, resident-capture and open-enrollment assumptions behind any merged-school projection, stated explicitly; (iii) a demonstration that the merged school fits the receiving building with margin under the upside cases, not only the median, net of the classroom footprint of center-based special-education programs; (iv) a school-specific accounting of one-time costs, recurring savings and revenue at risk from families who leave; and (v) a transition plan for RISE, school-age care, preschool and transportation.

**Enrollment** (attached appendix, sections 4–8). Between the January 2025 and January 2026 runs, the district's 2029-30 projection for Bear Creek rose from 272 to 310 and Mesa's fell from 224 to 202; its 2028-29 Bear Creek figure went 248, 274, 288 in three consecutive runs. The district's own January 2026 projections for the two schools add to 521 in 2030-31, above Bear Creek's 492 seats, so its range of 403–462 implies that only 41–71% of Mesa's projected students land at Bear Creek, an assumption the deck does not state. Across all BVSD elementary schools, one two-year-ahead projection in ten misses by more than 13% (n = 29). An independent model fitted to the district's grade-level counts, back-tested on 26 schools, finds one more October count would move the merged school's central estimate by roughly 50 students either way (trend specification). Most important, the district's Bear Creek projection for 2030-31 (320) matches the assumption that kindergarten intake holds at its post-2020 level (my model gives 319). Under that assumption the two schools together are about 522, over capacity, and the range of 403–462 is reachable only if 60 to 120 students go elsewhere; the chance the merged school exceeds the building is then 58%, against 16% if intake keeps falling.

**Interdependence** (section 9). Every projection, the district's and mine, is fitted on progression ratios and choice flows observed under a portfolio that will not exist after 2027-28, when three schools close, two focus schools move, boundaries are redrawn and new choice priorities apply. The identifiable channels point toward higher enrollment at Bear Creek, one of very few Boulder-region schools near three rounds; the district has not modeled them.

**Cost and benefit** (section 12). The deck states recurring savings of $3.5–4.0M a year for the whole package against one-time costs of $7.5–10M for facilities plus $5.0M for transition, a three-to-four-year payback if realized, with no Mesa-specific figure on either side. A statewide study of California districts, 2011–2019 (Pearman, Stanford, May 2026), finds closures reduced spending and revenue by nearly equal amounts ($447 and $433 per pupil, neither statistically significant), with no change in the odds of a balanced budget and no commensurate staff reduction, because closure regimes lost about 287 students each; the district's range assumes 29–59% of Mesa's projected students do not follow, which is that channel.

**Other gaps** (section 13). Five open questions:
- How many classrooms will AIM and RISE occupy at Bear Creek, and does 492 net them out?
- What school-age care capacity will Bear Creek offer relative to two schools' demand?
- Where will preschool be offered, given locations are promised only by Oct. 1?
- Where do the 29–59% of Mesa students the district's range assumes do not follow go, and what revenue leaves with them?
- Why was the K-2/3-5 reconfiguration, called "uncertain" in October, dropped without published scoring?

**The ask.** Decline to approve the Mesa/Bear Creek component of the Resilient Schools proposal at this time, and direct staff to return with an analysis that meets this standard.

The attached appendix is reproducible from public district documents, with a page citation for every number. I would be glad to walk any Board member or staff member through it before September 22.

Respectfully,

Tim Kautz
