**Subject:** Mesa/Bear Creek consolidation: the enrollment evidence does not yet support an irreversible decision

**To:** BVSD Board of Education (seven members)
**Cc:** Superintendent

Dear Board members,

I am the parent of a Mesa Elementary kindergartner. I am also a PhD economist and a senior researcher at Mathematica, and I am writing in a personal capacity; the views here are my own.

The district proposes an irreversible step on the basis of projections with no stated uncertainty. Its own projections for these two schools have moved 10–16% between annual runs, and one more October count could shift the merged school's expected enrollment by two classrooms in either direction. Keeping Mesa open is cheap and reversible; closing it is not.

I concede at the outset that Mesa is small, has been on the enrollment-advisory list every year it has existed, and that the district's central projection is not unreasonable. My concern is the evidence bar for an irreversible step.

**The standard.** Before approving any closure, the Board should have (i) a projection with stated prediction intervals, back-tested against the district's own October counts; (ii) the retention, resident-capture and open-enrollment assumptions behind any merged-school projection, stated explicitly; (iii) a demonstration that the merged school fits the receiving building with margin under the upside cases, not only the median, net of the classroom footprint of center-based special-education programs; (iv) a school-specific accounting of one-time costs, recurring savings, and revenue at risk from families who leave the district; and (v) a transition plan for RISE, school-age care, preschool and transportation.

**Enrollment** (attached appendix, sections 4–8). Between the January 2025 and January 2026 runs, the district's 2029-30 projection for Bear Creek rose from 272 to 310 and Mesa's fell from 224 to 202; Bear Creek's 2028-29 figure has gone 248, 274, 288 in three consecutive runs. The district's own January 2026 projections for the two schools add to 521 students in 2030-31, above Bear Creek's 492 seats, so its published range of 403–462 implies that only 41–71% of Mesa's projected students would actually land at Bear Creek, an assumption the deck does not state. Across all BVSD elementary schools, one two-year-ahead projection in ten misses by more than 13% (n = 29). An independent model fitted to the district's grade-level counts and back-tested on 26 schools finds that one more October count would move the merged school's central estimate by roughly 50 students in either direction. Most important, an undisclosed choice about whether kindergarten intake keeps falling or holds at its post-2020 level moves the 2030-31 median by about 71 students, more than the width of the district's entire range, and moves the probability that the merged school exceeds the building from 16% to 58%.

**Interdependence** (section 9). Every projection, the district's and mine, is fitted on progression ratios and choice flows observed under a portfolio that will not exist after 2027-28, when Douglass, Flatirons and Birch close, two focus schools move, boundaries are redrawn and new choice priorities apply. The identifiable channels point toward higher enrollment at Bear Creek, which becomes one of three Boulder schools near three rounds, and the district has not modeled them.

**Cost and benefit** (section 12). The deck states recurring savings of $3.5–4.0M a year for the whole package against one-time costs of $7.5–10M for facilities plus $5.0M for transition, a payback of three to four years if the savings are realized, with no Mesa-specific figure on either side. A statewide California study (Pearman, Stanford, May 2026) found that closures cut spending by about $447 per student but cut revenue by about $433 as families left; the district's own range assumes that 29–59% of Mesa's projected students do not follow, which is that channel.

**Other gaps** (section 13). Five questions the documents do not answer:
- How many classrooms will AIM and RISE occupy at Bear Creek, and does 492 net them out?
- What school-age care capacity will Bear Creek offer relative to two schools' current demand?
- Where will preschool be offered, given locations are promised only by Oct. 1, after the vote?
- How many Mesa-area addresses fall outside the 1.5-mile transportation radius for Bear Creek?
- Why was the K-2/3-5 reconfiguration, scored "uncertain" in October, dropped without published scoring?

**The ask.** Decline to approve the Mesa/Bear Creek component of the Resilient Schools proposal at this time, and direct staff to return with an analysis that meets this standard.

The attached technical appendix is fully reproducible from public district documents, with a page citation for every number. I would be glad to walk any Board member or staff member through it before September 22.

Respectfully,

Tim Kautz
