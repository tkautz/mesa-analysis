# QA review 3: plain-language reader (non-specialist parent), 2026-09-03

## 1. After reading only the email
Ask: don't approve closing Mesa on Sept 22; send staff back for a better analysis. Three reasons in my words: (1) the forecasts jump 10–16% year to year and nobody says how wrong they could be; (2) the district's own numbers for the two schools add to more kids than the building holds, and 403–462 only works if a lot of Mesa families leave, which it doesn't say; (3) no Mesa-specific cost figure. But the email gives a five-part standard, four bold sections and five bullets: fourteen things. Lost in the Enrollment paragraph (~200 words, ~20 numbers; "Most important" is the seventh sentence).

## 2. Read twice / numbers not understood / words not known

- "58%, against 16%": which am I supposed to believe? Never said.
- "fitted on progression ratios and choice flows observed under a portfolio": gave up.

- Numbers with no evident point: n = 29; 26 schools; $447/$433; 287; "my model gives 319". 41–71% and 29–59% look like two findings; they are one flipped.
- Words: back-tested, prediction intervals, resident-capture, net of, center-based, progression ratios, choice flows, portfolio, closure regimes, revenue at risk, K-2/3-5 reconfiguration, published scoring, AIM, RISE.

## 3. Voice
Consultant. Sentence one is a parent, sentence two a credential, then a scope-of-work. "I concede" is a lawyer's move. The kindergartner never comes back. Not a lobbyist (no emotion), which is a strength. Prosecutor's tic: four "you didn't do your homework" jabs in a row. Works: "Keeping Mesa open is cheap and reversible; closing it is not." and the offer to walk anyone through it.

## 4. Weakest points
a. Your own model says it probably fits (430, 16%). You headline 58%. Need one honest sentence: under one reasonable assumption it fits, under the other it doesn't, the district hasn't said which.
b. Overcrowding is a strange complaint from the school being closed; the board will say "we manage choice." Appendix answer (managing choice pulls it back to 2.5 rounds) is good and not in the email.
c. "Keeping Mesa open is cheap": asserted, not shown; two paragraphs later "no Mesa-specific figure on either side." Reads as special pleading.
d. "One more October count could shift by two classrooms" is true every year; Fig 11 shows waiting narrows 150 → 128 only. Need why this year is special (first count under new boundaries). Lead with it.
e. Uncertainty two-sided but only the upside argued.
f. "an assumption the deck does not state": the deck footnote states the dependence, not the share. Say it precisely.
g. Pearman: different state, formula, no significant results. Cut from email.
Most convincing: lead with the district's own arithmetic (521 / 492 / 403–462), one sentence on what waiting costs, then the ask.
