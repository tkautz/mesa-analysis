# QA review v2: district staff rebuttal to report.tex (2026-09-04)

Ratings = damage to the parent's case. Deck = Aug 25 deck; Feb = Feb 2026 report; June = June 9 update.

## Part 1. Rebuttals

**Finding 1 / Section 1, the bridge.**
(a) Decomposition. Any (residents, enrollment) pair implies a capture rate and an outside count, and the deck's footnote (p. 51) says the range turns on residents attending; a births-based resident method changes nothing. WEAK.
(b) Grandfathering. (i) The "duration of the school level" clause is in Policy JECC, not JECC-R, and continues "unless school assignment under this Policy necessitates adjustments to enrollment at particular schools or programs"; JECC also allows reassignment "if a school is overcrowded." (ii) Deck p. 63: in-district students at a closed school are "placed in their new neighborhood school," so Mesa's 42 non-resident OE/OOD students have no automatic seat at Bear Creek; they apply through OE, offers "depend[ing] on available space." Table 2 carries them until they age out. On the district's rules with zero new admissions and 83% capture: about 461 in 2027-28 (Bear Creek's 56 OE/OOD less one cohort, plus 23 placements) and 474 in 2030-31, against Table 2's 492/472: above 445/462 by 12-16, not 47. STRONG against "reachable only if capture falls"; "JECC-R allows only as students age out" is inaccurate.
(c) Placements: the 23 are district program placements (RISE/AIM) and persist, since all AIM/RISE programs move to Bear Creek (bvsd.org p. 7). Mislabeling that numerically hurts the district (manageable pool 98, not 121). WEAK; fix the label.
(d) "The projected rise does not come from any document" (App. B): the parent's own Section 4 (births 33 to 70) supplies the likely basis (Feb pp. 7-8). MODERATE.

**Finding 2 / Section 2, moving numbers; the August count.**
Revisions: Jan 2024 had Bear Creek at 287 for 2024-25 against a count of 318; the model updated, and Table 8 finds no bias. STRONG on framing; the band stands.
(c) August count: preliminary, live enrollment rather than CDE funded heads. In the same Aug 28 file, school-level gaps to the January run have median 4% and 90th percentile 10.4% (Sanchez -14%, Lafayette -13%, Pioneer +10%); Bear Creek's +10% is the largest but inside a wide August distribution, and Mesa is -6%. MODERATE: a flag, not a count; say "preliminary" in the finding. The pair at 534 vs 516 survives.

**Finding 3 / Section 3, rooms.**
1:24.58 (budget book p. 121) is an FTE allocation ratio, not a class-size cap: 480 students yields 19.5 classroom FTE, so 19-20 sections averaging 24-25; the ceilings are the BVEA goals (26/29/31, C-6), at which the parent's own Table 4 gives 19-21 sections, within 21 rooms in the central case. The headline "23 sections against 21 rooms" applies an average as a maximum. STRONG against the headline; MODERATE against the section, which prints both bases. "21 classrooms" is derived (3.5 x 6); the 2011-14 profile books list 18-19.

**Finding 4 / Section 4, the model and births.**
(d) The 2.5 entry ratio is K 84 (2024) over 33 births (2019), a trough denominator plus choice-in; a window containing it applied to the recovered 70 births double counts. District-wide K/births is 0.93 (Dec 2025 article), and BVSD uses births as the resident-K input with OE flows separate (Feb pp. 7-8), so "the district's own" method is a straw man; 2024 is one year in areas with 30-70 births (BC-Mesa 11 to 28). MODERATE; the direction claim stands.
"Risk is one-sided": over-capacity has reversible remedies (choice limits, boundaries; Meadowlark and Aspen Creek run 107-116%, Feb p. 9 footnote); Mesa at 1.4 rounds has none, and the parent never prices a year of delay. STRONG (framing).

**Finding 5 / Section 5, cost and process.**
(f) Site budget: staff agree most of the $3.02M follows students; the savings are per-school fixed positions and O&M, and $3.5-4.0M across six closed schools is $0.6-0.7M each, the parent's own $0.65M. WEAK. The Mesa bond project is in the parent's own source: FY27 budget book p. 207 lists Mesa at $1,384,757 ($42,034 spent; $24,000 proposed for 2026-27), Bear Creek $2,787,641; "some offset by savings from Bond projects" (p. 60) is that line, and the $5.0M is already "budgeted, FY27." MODERATE.
(e) Timeline: deck p. 70 prints "Sept. 8, 2026 - Present final plan for study to the Board" and "Sept. 22 - Present final plan for action": the Feb/June phases compressed by a month (also p. 3). The Sept 8 agenda was not due on Sept 3, so "none of it is on BoardDocs" is premature; Feb p. 13 already carried the boundaries/OE/facility language, so June "added" nothing. STRONG against the framing. Surviving: no reason for the compression; contents arrive at most 14 days before the vote.
"Least margin": true for 2030-31 by one seat over Superior (31); in 2027-28 Superior is at 96-98% (p. 37). MODERATE (selective). "The priority every range depends on": p. 63 governs where non-followers go, not the range. WEAK-MODERATE. K-2/3-5: June p. 32 records families' view that "grade splits are... structural deal breakers": a reason, though no fiscal comparison. MODERATE against App. F.

**Section 6, the ask.** Fairness: the same range method underlies all ten receivers; holding one component treats regions differently. STRONG (policy). Triggers: conceded (Nov 1 window, pp. 67, 70). The worksheet: unrebuttable.
